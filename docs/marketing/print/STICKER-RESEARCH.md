# Stickers, cards and posters: what real brands do

Research for the Syrmos print kit (`README.md`, `kit/`). Collected 2026-10-29. It complements `../3.0.2-GROWTH-RESEARCH.md`, which covers the legal rules, placement and printers.

**Verification key:** "Verified" means a fetched page states the detail. **[Unverified]** means it came only from a search snippet, a secondary summary, or my own inference. A brand with nothing verifiable is listed as a gap; I did not fill gaps with guesses.

**Headline finding:** across 11 brands I found no public sticker spec (size, copy and QR together) that I could verify. What is verifiable is the split below, and every successful example follows it:

- **Keepers:** mascot, mark or joke, no QR, no URL, no app UI (Duolingo, BVG, TfL Go roundels, RATP illustrations, GitHub Octocats).
- **Scanners:** a QR with a specific job, placed where that job happens, and copy saying what the scan does (TfL 2011 countdown codes, Google's 2009 decal, Moovit's bus-door validator, OASA's stop codes).

## 4. Kept on a laptop vs thrown away

Verified principles:

- **The test:** "Would you use this sticker if it were for someone else's brand?" People use stickers that "reflect them, not just you." ([Stomp Stickers](https://stompstickers.com/blogs/articles/5-tips-for-designing-logo-stickers-people-will-actually-use))
- **Size and shape:** 2 to 3 inches (5 to 7.5 cm) is the "sweet spot". Die-cut to the shape beats a rectangle with white space around it. Remove text. (same source; [TechBullion](https://techbullion.com/why-custom-laptop-stickers-remain-one-of-the-smartest-branding-moves-in-tech/), a weaker source, adds "Skip URLs and marketing copy")
- **Identity:** a study of about 140 student laptops found raters could read extraversion and openness from stickers alone. Stickers are self-advertising ([UF News, 2022](https://news.ufl.edu/2022/03/what-your-laptop-stickers-say-about-you-/)). Categories people use: political or social, subcultural, professional, aesthetic ([Ross](https://jeffreyianross.com/the-semiotics-of-laptop-computer-covers/)).
- **Open source:** stickers signal support for a cause. "Your project either needs to be huge, or you should have the project's name on the sticker somehow." Stickers earned by contributing carry extra value ([opensource.com](https://opensource.com/business/15/11/open-source-stickers)).
- **Finish:** matte for laptops (looks premium, hides fingerprints); gloss is UV-protected and better on outdoor bottles ([StickerApp](https://stickerapp.com/blog/sticker-academy/glossy-vs-matte-stickers-differences-and-areas-of-use)).
- **Hexagon standard:** 5.08 cm across the largest diagonal, vertex at the top ([sticker.how](https://sticker.how/)). It fits neatly next to dev stickers; not required.

What gets thrown away (inferred from the above, **[my synthesis]**): rectangles with a URL, a QR and a slogan; anything that only says "download"; items too big for a laptop lid or too small to read.

### QR rules

- **Distance:** about 10:1 scan distance to code size, 2 x 2 cm minimum ([QR Planet](https://qrplanet.com/help/article/what-is-the-minimum-size-of-a-qr-code), [QR Code Generator](https://www.qr-code-generator.com/blog/qr-code-distance/)). NN/g: add 1 cm per extra 10 cm of distance ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/qr-code-guidelines/)).
- **Quiet zone:** 4 modules (vendors cite ISO/IEC 18004; I did not read the ISO text) **[primary source unverified]**.
- **Copy:** "Tell users what a QR code does and where it came from" (NN/g). A 3 to 5 word CTA starting with a verb ([QR Code Generator](https://www.qr-code-generator.com/blog/call-to-action-qr-codes/)). TfL 2011 confirms it in practice.

## 5. Multilingual tourist transit print

- **Tokyo, 2015 guideline (verified, PDF):** signs carry Japanese and English plus pictograms. Chinese and Korean are added only where needed and legible. **Paper handouts and digital tools carry more languages, flexibly.** A shared 1,800-term glossary keeps names identical across operators. ([PDF](https://www.sangyo-rodo.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/tourism/signs/pdf/01.pdf))
- **Tokyo Metro station numbering:** letter plus colour plus number (Ginza G09), added in 2004 "specifically for foreign visitors and passengers with limited literacy". Codes beat translation. ([Desirability Lab](https://desirabilitylab.com/backfill/backfill-2021-52-tokyo-metro-wayfinding))
- **Tokyo in-car displays:** languages rotate in time (kanji, hiragana, roman), not stacked in space. ([Language on the Move](https://languageonthemove.com/tokyo-elegantly-multilingual/))
- **Lisbon Metro:** a separate English-only A3 fares sheet, one language per piece, with short imperatives: "Tap your card, not your wallet." ([PDF](https://www.metrolisboa.pt/en/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/12/Tarifario_dez_2024_UK_site-1.pdf))
- **Heathrow Express:** English only, one long line on a 19 m wall. Not a multilingual model. ([creative.salon](https://creative.salon/articles/work/st-lukes-heathrow-express-tracks))
- **Flags vs language names:** no fetched source discusses it. **[Gap]**
- **Not found:** Barcelona TMB, Copenhagen, Wiener Linien, Paris tourist leaflets, Arlanda Express, Swiss Travel System (per-language editions seen only in snippets).

## 7. Ten recommendations for Syrmos

Formats: 8 cm vinyl sticker, A6 card, A3 poster, in EN, EL, SQ, IT. Each one says which current kit item it affects.

**Status (2026-10-29):** 2, 3, 5 and 6 are applied in `kit/build_kit.py` and covered by `kit/test_kit.py`. 7, 8, 9 and 10 describe what the kit already did. 4 (owl expressions) is open.

**Owner decision, same day, overriding 1:** every sticker carries a QR, and that QR holds a short offline guide to its line (plain text from the seed, ending in the tracked link), not just a link. The research's concern was that a QR makes a sticker look like an ad. A guide that is useful with no signal answers part of that, since the code now gives something rather than asks for an install. Stickers are printed in English, Greek and Albanian. Watch the `st1` to `st7` scan counts and how many stickers people actually take, to see whether keepability suffers.

1. **Split keepers from scanners, and take the QR off the sticker face.** Every keeper found (Duolingo, Octocats, BVG, TfL Go) has no QR; every working QR is on a separate piece with a job. The current ST design is a 61 mm badge plus a QR stub. Move the QR onto the kiss-cut sheet margin and the liner print, or make the stub a separate kiss-cut piece that peels off. The badge then works as an 8 cm sticker someone would put on a laptop. Attribution still works, because the sheet is what you hand over.
2. **Keep the 8 cm sticker die-cut, matte, 1 to 3 words.** Die-cut to the roundel shape, matte laminated vinyl for laptops. Use gloss only for a bottle variant. Text is a line code plus at most one short phrase. No URL, no tagline, no disclaimer on the face (the kit already moves the disclaimer to the liner).
3. **Make the set the hook: numbered, local, collectible.** The six-piece "Lines of Greece" 1/6 to 6/6 set fits the Octocat and Duo pattern of variants people collect. Give each sticker a symbol and colour, not a translated sentence (Tokyo's codes beat translation). Add a seventh "contributor" variant that is only given to people who file a bug, add a translation or star the repo. Stickers earned by contributing carry extra value (opensource.com).
4. **Use the owl as Syrmos's Duo, with expressions instead of slogans.** Duolingo's pack is one character in 15 moods. A small set of owl moods tied to real commute moments (waiting, running, tunnel with no signal, last train) is a sticker people pick for themselves. **[Inference from Duolingo; untested for Syrmos]**
5. **The QR CTA should say what the scan gives, not "get the app".** The kit's current line is "Scan to get the app". NN/g and TfL 2011 both say the copy should state what the code does. Applied as "Scan for your next train" / "Σκάναρε για τον επόμενο συρμό" / "Skano për trenin e ardhshëm" / "Inquadra per il prossimo treno", reusing the app's own "next train" strings. SQ and IT still need a native speaker, as the README says for all SQ and IT copy.
6. **Size QR codes for the real scan distance, with 1.5x margin.** A6 card on a counter is scanned at 30 to 40 cm, so 4 cm (current) is fine. An A3 poster in a café window may be scanned from 1 to 1.5 m, so make it 12 to 15 cm instead of 10 cm (applied: 12 cm). Keep dark on light, 4-module quiet zone, error correction M, and the short URL printed under the code (all already in the kit).
7. **One language per piece, and label it with the language's own name, not a flag.** Tokyo and Lisbon both put many languages on separate print pieces, not stacked on one. The kit already does this. When a piece lists other languages ("Also in: Ελληνικά · Shqip · Italiano"), use the language names. A flag stands for a country, not a language, and an Albanian speaker may be from Kosovo or North Macedonia **[my reasoning; no fetched source covers flags]**. The 2009 ministry guide is a precedent for printing Albanian in Athens.
8. **The A6 card sells one concrete moment, with UI, like Transit's copy blocks.** The card is the "scanner", so it may show the app's real UI (Transit's partner kit uses a UI image and three short benefit lines). Keep the current "Flight at 10? Leave at 7:00." plus the notification. Cap it at three benefits, and make "Free · Open source · No ads" one of them, because it is the line no competitor can print.
9. **The A3 poster leads with a mark or a joke, not a screenshot.** BVG self-irony, RATP illustrations and the TfL Go roundels all win attention without a phone screenshot. Keep the UI small and below the headline. Lead with one big human line, such as the current "Your train in 3 min.", and the line colours. Do not copy BVG's self-mockery: Syrmos is not the operator, and mocking STASY or OASA would read as hostile.
10. **Stay clearly separate from official stop QR codes and signage.** OASA has QR plates at about 400 stops that open OASA Telematics. A Syrmos code near a stop would look like a fake official plate, and placing it there is illegal anyway (growth research, section 1). Keep Syrmos print in permission-based venues, keep the "Not affiliated with STASY, OASA, OSETH or Hellenic Train" line on every card and poster, and avoid the Thessaloniki Metro "μ" mark and STASY roundel layouts. Measure each piece with its own `src` code, as Transit does with per-agency tracked links.
